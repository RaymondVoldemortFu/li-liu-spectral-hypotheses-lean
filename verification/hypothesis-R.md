# Statement and proof audit for Hypothesis R

Theorem 1.1 of Leiming Fu’s manuscript gives cofinal lower bounds for the operator norm, refutes Li–Liu’s Hypothesis R and constructs one sequence of real spectral zeros approaching one faster than every fixed inverse power of the logarithm. The Lean theorem `GoldbachSpectral435.RPaperAudit.paper_theorem` states all three conclusions together. It takes no hypotheses. [Manuscript](../paper/R/refutation-of-R.pdf) · [Formal statement](../WithGRH435/RPaperAudit.lean)

## What is being negated

Li and Liu define local GRH on page 3 and R on page 4. The quantifiers are one cutoff, then every even modulus beyond it satisfying local GRH, then every quaternionic zero. The local condition ranges over primitive Dirichlet characters whose positive conductor divides the modulus. Equations (1.5) and (1.6) give the scale and the spectral product. Theorem 6.1 on page 35 gives the norm criterion at one eligible modulus. Appendix A on page 53 fixes the main text’s strip parameter at $c=1$. [Original PDF](https://mrc.sdu.edu.cn/__local/6/14/8E/28753731EE31D7635BAF60732C2_0C644604_2CE958.pdf) · [Pinned Lean definition](https://github.com/subfish-zhou/conditional-goldbach-lean/blob/c55db645f3b4aed00374fe4aa1fcee2428c88c4b/formalization/ConditionalGoldbach/Definitions/Hypotheses.lean)

The audit spells out the cutoff, parity, conductor divisibility, L-function zeros in the open critical strip, the quaternionic variable and the strip endpoints $1/\log N$ and $1/8$. Lean proves by reflexivity that the resulting proposition is both `HypothesisR` and `HypothesisRAt 1`. The verified scale satisfies $\delta_N^2=\mathfrak S(N)(\log N)^3/N$. For eligible moduli, `realization` proves that the operator represents the weighted kernel and that the explicitly written finite quaternionic products converge to the spectral function used in R. This checks the existence conditions in the library’s definitions by classical choice. [Statement, scale and realization checks](../WithGRH435/RPaperAudit.lean)

## How the proof reaches the conclusion

| Step in the manuscript | What the formal proof uses |
| --- | --- |
| The mixed matrix element | Use modulus $N$ and sampling scale $2N$. Polarizing at $f+g,f,g$ cancels the diagonal and the signed beta convolution on the half-integer lattice. Odd-character orthogonality isolates the samples at $N\pm m$. The signed projection is $-B_N/(2N)$. The norm estimate gives $H B_N^2\le C P^2 N\lVert T_N\rVert^2$. [Quadratic identity](../Analytic435/Quadratic.lean) · [Mixed terms](../Analytic435/Entry.lean) · [Transfer](../Analytic435/Opposite.lean) |
| The odd sieve layer | Finite inclusion–exclusion leaves a positive contribution in the top odd layer. The ordinary prime number theorem supplies a uniform lower bound over the macroscopic intervals. The residue row has size $\gg_k t^{3k}$ for each fixed odd $k$. [Layer identity](../SpectralTests/BandSieve.lean) · [Prime mass](../SpectralTests/BandMass.lean) · [Row bound](../SpectralTests/Parameters.lean) |
| Transfer to an even modulus | Mellin inversion and the contour shift from $1$ to $1/4$ bound the nonprincipal character sums. Orthogonality gives the $M^{3/4}Q^2$ progression error. Averaging over centres produces an even $N$ and a large opposite-shift sum. Local GRH is supplied at the auxiliary modulus $Q$ and at the target modulus $N$. [Contour estimate](../Analytic435/Characters.lean) · [Progressions](../Analytic435/PhysicalAP.lean) · [Shift errors](../Analytic435/ShiftMean.lean) · [Modulus selection](../Analytic435/AtWindow.lean) |
| Norm growth and R | For fixed $d$, choose odd $k$ with $2k>9d$. At arbitrarily large even moduli, $N\lVert T_N\rVert^2>(\log N)^d$. R would give $N\lVert T_N\rVert^2\le\mathfrak S(N)(\log N)^3\le(\log N)^5$ eventually. Taking $d=5$ gives the contradiction. [Growth and refutation](../Analytic435/SpectralRefutation.lean) · [Original norm criterion](../Analytic435/OriginalBounds.lean) |
| One real zero sequence | For each $j$, an eventual zero-free width $1/(4(\log N)^j)$ would contradict the lower bound with $d=j+6$. The quadratic factor makes a zero in that region real and places it below one. Choosing successively larger moduli for $j=i$ gives one sequence for every fixed exponent. [Width obstruction](../SpectralTests/Obstruction.lean) · [Original zeros and diagonal selection](../Analytic435/OriginalStatements.lean) |

The manuscript writes the analytic kernel with the beta expression. Lean defines the residual kernel using the analytically continued model. The two expressions agree on the beta domain, which contains the pairs used on the Hardy strip. The proof uses the zeta functional equation and gamma identities. The analytic transfer, prime-number input and progression estimates all have proof bodies.
